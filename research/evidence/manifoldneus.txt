ManifoldNeuS: Manifold-aware View Optimizability for Pose-Free Neural

Abstract
Jointly optimizing camera poses and object geometry
from unposed images is a challenging task in neural sur-
face reconstruction. Existing methods often suffer from pose
drift and geometric distortion, stemming from the easy-view
bias — uniform view optimization favors easy-to-optimize
views with abundant texture and good overlap that domi-
nate gradient updates, while hard-to-optimize counterparts
with weak texture or limited overlap yet critical for geo-
metric completeness are progressively marginalized. To ad-
dress this, we propose ManifoldNeuS, a novel method that
explicitly models and leverages per-view optimizability to
guide pose-free neural surface reconstruction. Specifically,
we introduce the manifold-aware view optimizability score
(MaVOS), which jointly assesses immediate fitness (the ease
of optimizing each view) and long-term coverage gain (the
value of optimizing each view) over the view-coherent man-
ifold. Building on the MaVOS, we further devise a recon-
struction pipeline that incorporates the per-view optimiz-
ability as a state control signal to guide the joint optimiza-
tion process through three key components: dynamic view
scheduling, gated positional encoding, and anti-score loss
weighting. Experimental results on the benchmark dataset
demonstrate that ManifoldNeuS outperforms existing meth-
ods in terms of accurate pose estimation and high-quality
reconstruction, achieving robust joint optimization without
known camera poses.
1. Introduction
Multi-view 3D reconstruction is a cornerstone problem
in computer vision and graphics. Recently, remarkable
progress has been achieved in 3D reconstruction using neu-
ral radiance fields (NeRFs) [42] and 3D Gaussian Splat-
ting (3DGS) [29]. However, these methods typically
assume access to accurate camera poses, usually esti-
mated by Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines such as

COLMAP [49], which limits their practical implementa-
tion when pose estimates are noisy or unavailable. Conse-
quently, jointly optimizing camera poses and scene geome-
try has attracted considerable attention.
Despite it being a severely ill-posed optimization prob-
lem, recent efforts have explored various methodologies to
tackle this challenge. Methods based on 3DGS achieve re-
markable rendering quality, yet their explicit, discrete rep-
resentation fundamentally conflicts with the thin nature of
surfaces, limiting their ability to recover intricate geome-
try [22, 69]. Moreover, 3DGS-based joint optimization re-
lies on additional well-initialized point clouds, increasing
sensitivity to camera parameters [18]. Methods based on
Transformer frameworks leverage strong data-driven priors
to regress geometry and poses directly, but generally require
large-scale datasets with ground-truth poses for supervi-
sion [6]. In contrast, neural implicit methods enable contin-
uous surface representations well-suited for high-fidelity re-
construction via enforcing multi-view consistency and dif-
ferentiable volume rendering [17, 34].
While previous works have explored joint optimization
of camera poses and geometry within implicit representa-
tion frameworks [2, 23, 33, 48], they typically optimize
all views uniformly, leading to a critical yet underexplored
challenge, which we termeasy-view bias. This bias man-
ifests in two intertwined dimensions that destabilize joint
optimization: First, local gradient dominance causes highly
overlapping views to produce disproportionately strong gra-
dients early in the optimization. Consequently, these “easy
views” quickly lock their camera poses while dominating
geometry updates, starving weakly-textured regions of es-
sential gradient signals. Second, and more fundamentally,
global coverage neglect arises when optimization favors
immediate photometric accuracy over long-term geometric
completeness. Candidate views covering previously unob-
served regions provide critical topological value that out-
weighs their short-term optimization difficulty. Marginaliz-
ing them forces pose drift, resulting in the reconstruction of
fragments, rather than the lack of local details.

view bias by fundamentally modeling the optimizability of

idea is that a view’s optimizability, namely whether it is
both easy and worthwhile to optimize, is determined by
both its immediate fitness,i.e., how effectively it accel-
erates convergence and refines existing geometry, and its
long-term coverage gain,i.e., how much it expands topolog-
ical coverage and helps recover unknown geometry. Cru-
cially, evaluating coverage gain requires modeling topolog-
ical relationships among views, where Euclidean metrics
are insufficient. To formalize this insight, we introduce
the manifold-aware view optimizability score (MaVOS), a
novel metric that unifies both local constraint strength and
global topological coverage. The former quantifies im-
mediate fitness through feature matching, while the latter
measures long-term coverage gain via minimum distances
over an embedded view graph. Building on MaVOS, we
propose a MaVOS-guided reconstruction method to trans-
form per-view optimizability into overall optimization sta-
bility, including view scheduling with dynamic refinement
to guide progressive optimization from local to global,
MaVOS-gated positional encoding to prevent premature
high-frequency interference on poses by modulating fre-
quency bands, and anti-score loss weighting to rebalance
gradient contributions across views.
In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
1. We identify the easy-view bias in pose-free neural sur-
face reconstruction: uniform view optimization favors
easy-to-optimize views while overlooking those valu-
able topology-enhancing ones, leading to pose drift and
geometric distortion.
2. We introduce a manifold-aware view optimizability
score that unifies local constraint strength and global
topological coverage to quantify immediate fitness and
long-term topological gain.
3. We develop a MaVOS-guided reconstruction pipeline
that transforms per-view optimizability into overall opti-
mization stability, thereby enhancing 3D reconstruction
quality from unposed views.
2. Related Work
Scene representations for 3D reconstruction.Ad-
vances in multi-view 3D reconstruction are driven by three
paradigms: transformer-based, 3DGS-based, and neural im-
plicit methods. Transformer-based methods [6, 31, 50, 53,
56, 57], like DUST3R [57] and VGGT [53], enable feed-
forward and calibration-free reconstruction by directly re-
gressing 3D structure and poses, but require extensive su-
pervised training on pose-annotated data; in contrast, we
recover geometry and poses from unposed images without
pose supervision. 3DGS [29] provides an explicit repre-
sentation for high-quality novel view synthesis, modeling
scenes as anisotropic Gaussians optimized via a Gaussian
rasterizer. Despite extensions [4, 13, 21, 22, 38, 67, 69] tar-
geting surface reconstruction, its discrete and unstructured
point representation is primarily optimized for appearance
rather than geometric regularization, yielding noisy and
fragmented surfaces. In unposed settings, 3DGS faces addi-
tional challenges due to its dependency on well-initialized
point clouds. Most methods thus utilize external priors,
such as off-the-shelf geometric predictors [9, 11, 15, 66]
or depth estimators [18, 32] for initialization, yet are unre-
liable without ground-truth poses and typically restrict ap-
plicability to ordered data [25, 26, 28, 61]. Consequently,
few works have demonstrated robust surface reconstruction
from unposed images within the 3DGS framework.
Neural implicit methods, initiated by NeRF [42], model
appearance and geometry as continuous functions. Sub-
sequent works, like UNISURF [43], V olSDF [65], and
NeuS [55], advance geometric modeling by incorporat-
ing implicit occupancy fields or signed distance functions
(SDFs) via volume rendering. Among them, SDF-based
methods [17, 34, 36, 47, 55, 59, 65, 68] extract the surface
as the zero-level set of SDF using the Marching Cubes al-
gorithm [37], producing stronger geometric fidelity [48]. In
this work, we build our framework upon neural implicit rep-
resentations, particularly NeuS, owing to its surface fidelity.
Pose-free neural implicit representation.To eliminate re-
liance on precomputed poses, recent work jointly optimizes
camera poses and neural scene representations with poses
as learnable parameters. Early methods [5, 12, 60, 63] han-
dle only forward-facing scenes; subsequent works support
complex trajectories but require accurate pose priors, ei-
ther near-ground-truth initializations [7, 16, 35, 44, 62] or
known distributions [10, 39, 40, 70, 71]. Recent attempts
adopt identity initialization to remove priors, yet suffer from
local minima, motivating geometric cues (e.g.monocu-
lar depth, optical flow) [3, 24, 54] or incremental SLAM-
inspired incremental registration [41, 45, 58, 64, 72].
The aforementioned methods target novel view synthe-
sis; neural surface reconstruction demands far stricter pose
accuracy. Minor misalignments propagate to severe geo-
metric distortion, as SDFs require highly consistent multi-
view constraints to reach the correct zero level set. Meth-
ods like PoRF [2] and SC-NeuS [23] handle noisy poses
yet rely on coarse pose estimates, leaving truly pose-free
surface reconstruction under-explored. NoPose-NeuS [48]
leverages monocular-depth-supervised feature consistency;
ParaSurRe [33] employs cluster-wise incremental recon-
struction with PnP-based SDF fusion. However, both adopt
uniform training, treating all views equally regardless of
per-view optimizability differences. We address this by
quantifying per-view optimizability to guide pose-free neu-
ral surface reconstruction.

3. Method
Given a collection of unposed images, our goal is to re-
cover camera poses and neural surface geometry simulta-
neously. To address easy-view bias induced by uniform
optimization in pose-free neural surface reconstruction, we
propose a comprehensive method centered on the manifold-
aware view optimizability score (MaVOS) (Sec. 3.2), as il-
lustrated in Fig. 1. MaVOS quantifies per-view optimiz-
ability via local constraint strength and, crucially, globally
topological coverage measured on a view-coherent mani-
fold approximated from multi-view geometric consistency.
This manifold-aware design yields a balanced MaVOS that
jointly accounts for the ease and value of optimizing each
view. We then integrate MaVOS into the optimization pro-
cess via three complementary components (Sec. 3.3), which
transform per-view optimizability into a state-aware control
signal within the NeuS framework, enabling stable and ac-
curate joint optimization from unposed images.

3.3. MaVOS-Guided Reconstruction
We apply this unified scoring to make the optimization
view-aware within the NeuS framework. First, MaVOS-
based view scheduling establishes a dynamic optimiza-
tion sequence: we initialize reconstruction with a set
of high-MaVOS views as anchors, then continuously re-
order remaining views based on evolving MaVOS evalu-
ations, enabling stable local-to-global convergence. Sec-
ond, MaVOS-gated positional encoding adaptively mod-
ulates activation of frequency bases, specifically delaying
high-frequency components for high-MaVOS views to pre-
vent premature detail fitting from destabilizing pose esti-
mation. Third, anti-score loss weighting calibrates gradient
contributions by assigning higher weights to low-MaVOS
views. Collectively, these components transform per-view
optimizability into a state-aware optimization control signal
for a stable joint optimization.
View scheduling with dynamic refinement.Our view
scheduling strategy implements a progressive optimization
paradigm: first establishing a geometrically stable core
through reliable anchor views, then incrementally expand-
ing reconstruction coverage through dynamically selecting
remaining views.
We initialize reconstruction with the top-Kviews ranked
by MaVOS values to form the anchor setA. The parame-
terKis determined by the local constraint strengthσ i, as
a highσ i value guarantees sufficient pairwise feature corre-
spondences, which are essential for stabilizing initial pose
and geometry estimation.

4.2. Comparisons with the State-of-the-art Methods
We report quantitative results on camera pose accuracy in
Table 1 and visualize them in Fig. 2. It can be observed
that NeuS-BARF suffers catastrophic failure when poses di-
verge, indicating that naive adoption of BARF within neu-
ral implicit representations is ineffective. This highlights
that SDF-based neural implicit methods face greater chal-
lenges in pose estimation than novel view synthesis, mak-
ing it highly sensitive to initial pose inaccuracies and prone
to optimization collapse. SG-NeuS is designed for noisy
pose reconstruction where inputs are close to the ground
truth. Remarkably, our method, initialized to the identity,
achieves lower errors, demonstrating superior performance
in pose-free surface reconstruction. HT-3DGS exhibits rel-
atively large pose errors, as with most 3DGS-based pose-
free methods, because it relies on inter-frame cues and is
thus constrained to video-stream scenes. Fundamentally,
3DGS-based approaches require not only accurate poses

5. Conclusion
We present ManifoldNeuS, a novel method for joint camera
pose and geometry optimization from unposed images. By
discovering and addressing the cause of pose drift and ge-
ometric distortion,i.e., the easy-view-bias in uniform view
optimization, we introduce the manifold-aware view opti-
mizability score (MaVOS). This metric uniquely balances
local constraint strength and global topological coverage to
quantify how effectively each view advances optimization.
Building on MaVOS, our reconstruction pipeline integrates
three synergistic components, including view scheduling,
positional encoding, and loss weighting. Our experiments
show that ManifoldNeuS achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance in both pose estimation and geometry reconstruction.
We believe that our method encourages further development
of pose-free neural surface reconstruction in the future by
introducing view optimizability.
