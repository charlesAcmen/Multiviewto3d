Abstract
This work aims to reconstruct the 3D geometry of a rigid
object manipulated by one or both hands using monocu-
lar RGB video. Previous methods rely on Structure-from-
Motion or hand priors to estimate relative motion between
the object and camera, which typically assume textured ob-
jects or single-hand interactions. To accurately recover
object geometry in dynamic interactions, we incorporate
priors from 3D generation model into object pose estima-
tion and propose semantic consistency constraints to solve
the challenge of shape and texture discrepancy between
the generated priors and observations. The poses are ini-
tialized, followed by joint optimization of the object poses
and implicit neural representation. During optimization,
a novel pose outlier voting strategy with inter-view consis-
tency is proposed to correct large pose errors. Experiments
on three datasets demonstrate that our method significantly
outperforms the state-of-the-art in reconstruction quality
for both single- and two-hand scenarios. Our project page:
https://east-j.github.io/dynhor/
1. Introduction
Modeling the 3D shapes of objects has numerous applica-
tions in fields such as virtual/augmented reality and robotic
manipulation. Compared to the common approach of cap-
*Corresponding author.
turing static objects by manually moving a camera around
them, rotating the object by hand in front of a camera or
head-mounted device offers greater convenience for appli-
cations involving dynamic hand-object interactions. This
approach also enables reconstruction of the entire object
surface, including the bottom part. This hand-held object
reconstruction problem is the focus of the work.
Recent hand-held object reconstruction methods gener-
ally adopt multi-view reconstruction techniques [49, 54]
to aggregate observations of in-hand objects across multi-
ple rigid poses. The key challenge lies in accurately es-
timating the relative motion between the object and the
camera. Some works [18, 21, 42] assume the object is
firmly grasped, tracking object motion with a hand pose
tracker instead. This constrains hand-object movement and
causes parts of the object to be consistently occluded by the
hand, requiring post-processing or prior knowledge to ob-
tain a complete surface, which limits reconstruction qual-
ity and the potential application scenarios. Another line
of method [9] uses Structure-from-Motion (SfM) to initial-
ize object poses and refine them with hand priors. How-
ever, as pointed out by [13, 43], SfM struggles in dynamic
scenes. The solution is also limited to single-hand interac-
tions due to using hand priors. To overcome these limita-
tions, Hampali et al. [13] proposed an incremental training
approach that divides sequences into trackable segments for
joint shape and pose optimization. While effective for two-
hand interactions, this approach struggles with texture-less

or thin-structured objects and suffers from pose errors at

Towards enabling accurate reconstruction of a rigid ob-
ject manipulated dynamically by one or both hands, we pro-
pose a novel hand-held object reconstruction pipeline con-
sisting of pose initialization with generated 3D object priors
and joint optimization for object poses and reconstruction
with pose outlier voting. For methods using hand priors, the
high-DOF deformable hand itself is hard to resolve and not
robust in challenging environments. Additionally, occlu-
sions and dynamic objects make SfM-based methods brittle.
Instead, recent 3D object generation models can compen-
sate for occluded information for hand-held object recon-
struction and eliminate the unstable high-DOF deformable
part from the algorithm.
For pose initialization, our approach generates object
prior from the pre-trained 3D generation model to constrain
the pose space, eliminating the need for SfM or hand priors.
One major challenge of utilizing the generated 3D models is
the shape and texture discrepancy from the ground truth: the
discrepancy can cause registration methods that minimize
the appearance difference between a frame and the rendered
counterpart from the model to fail easily. We propose using
semantic consistency to address the appearance discrepancy
in shape and texture for pose initialization with generated
3D models, e.g. the distance between caps of bottles in dif-
ferent sizes, shapes, and textures in the semantic manifold
should be smaller than the distance between the caps and
the bottle bottoms. Semantic consistency also enables pose
estimation for poorly textured and thin-structured objects.
With the initialized poses, we jointly optimize the neural
implicit representation of the object and the poses during the
interaction. While joint optimization can refine the poses in
many cases, it struggles with frames that have severe oc-
clusions between the hand and the object due to significant
deviations in the initial poses. These outlier poses lead to
incorrect optimization and hinder pose self-correction. To
address this problem, we propose a pose outlier voting strat-
egy to check inter-view consistency with Sampson distance
in the local windows of input sequences. The detected out-
liers are reset with poses propagated from neighboring in-
liers during optimization.
We evaluate our method on three datasets: HO3D [12],
HOD [18], and HOPE [47], which contain objects with di-
verse geometries and textures interacting with one or both
hands. The experiments show that our approach signifi-
cantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of re-
construction quality and demonstrate its real-world appli-
cability through in-hand scanning with a mobile phone, as
shown in Fig. 1. To summarize our contributions:
• We propose a novel method for hand-held object re-
construction under dynamic interaction with one or both
hands using priors from pre-trained 3D generation model;
• We propose a frame-to-model alignment method with se-
mantic consistency to address the appearance discrepancy
in shape and texture between the generated 3D priors and
the input RGB sequences;
• We propose a pose outlier voting strategy that leverages
Sampson distance to check inter-frame pose consistency,
ensuring both pose accuracy and reconstruction quality.
2. Related Work
Text-to-3D. Text-to-3D generation creates 3D assets from
text prompts. Building on advances in text-to-image syn-
thesis with diffusion models [16], DreamFusion [34] adapts
image-based diffusion models [38] for 3D generation. Sub-
sequent work has improved generation quality, training sta-
bility, and speed [29, 31, 41, 44]. Another approach [17,
22, 26, 52] uses Large Reconstruction Models to generate
3D structures in a feed-forward manner. Building on these
advances, we can generate high-quality meshes from text to
guide coarse pose initialization.
Multi-view 3D Reconstruction. Recovering 3D geome-
try from multi-view images has a long history in computer
vision and graphics. Traditional approaches typically uti-
lize Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [39] to estimate camera
poses, followed by multi-view stereo (MVS) to generate
dense 3D reconstructions using explicit representations [3,
11, 25]. Recently, neural implicit representations [32] have
effectively modeled 3D geometry and appearance through
volume rendering. NeuS [49] and V olSDF [54] enhance sur-
face quality by replacing density fields with signed distance
fields. While these methods achieve high-quality recon-
structions, they rely on accurate pose estimates, typically
obtained with COLMAP [40]. However, COLMAP as-
sumes static scenes, making it unsuitable for scenarios like
ours, where hand-object movement is independent of the
background. Recent works [20, 28] address this by jointly
optimizing poses and implicit representations with added
supervision from correspondences [35, 46] or monocular
depth [2], but they are effective only in forward-facing sce-
narios. In contrast, we approach this problem by proposing
effective initial poses, a critical factor for successful joint
shape-pose optimization [50].
Hand-held Object Reconstruction. Reconstructing 3D
manipulated objects is challenging due to occlusions and
object diversity. Several methods [5, 53] rely on ground-
truth CAD models, simplifying the task to 6DoF pose es-
timation. Other learning-based approaches attempt to di-
rectly infer representations (e.g., mesh [6, 14], SDF [7, 23,
56]) from a single RGB(D) image. Unfortunately, these
methods suffer from poor generalization due to limited 3D
hand-object data, and the reconstructed results often lack
detail. CHORD [27] leverages categorical object priors for
improvement but remains constrained to narrow category
ranges. Recent advancements [9, 18, 21, 42, 57, 58] use

3. Methods
An overview of our method is illustrated in Fig. 2. Given
a monocular RGB video sequence {Ik}k=1,...,N of a hand
(or both hands) interacting with a rigid object, our goal is
to reconstruct the target 3D object shape. The key idea is
to use generated 3D meshes V as the priors to obtain the
initial object poses ˆξ in the input frames for the reconstruc-
tion. More specifically, we first generate V based on the
text description. As shown in Fig. 2, although there may
be discrepancy in shape and texture between the generated
3D mesh and input and directly aligning them under the
discrepancy is challenging, the generated mesh remains se-
mantically similar and can help to constrain the pose space.
To this end, we estimate object pose ˆξk for the kth frame
by aligning the input image Ik with renderings in the foun-
dational semantic-feature space FDIN O (Sec. 3.2). Sub-
sequently, we use initial poses {ˆξk}k=1,...,N to jointly op-
timize the SDF-based object implicit representation Φ and
poses (Sec. 3.3). To reduce the impact of poses with signif-
icant errors, we introduce a pose outlier voting strategy that
detects and reinitializes these poses (Sec. 3.4), ultimately
yielding higher-quality geometry and more accurate poses.

3.2. Pose Initialization via Generated 3D Prior and
Semantic Consistency
Generating a textured mesh V as the prior for pose initial-
ization takes three steps: (1) We prompt ChatGPT [1] to
describe the shape and appearance of the hand-held object
using the first frame I1 1; (2) The response of ChatGPT
serves as the input prompt for the Text-to-3D model, with
Genie [45] as the generator, producing high-quality 3D tex-
tured meshes; (3) We generateNV meshes and compute the
3D-image similarity between each mesh and all frames us-
ing OpenShape [30], selecting the mesh with the highest
score as V . This approach enables fully automated mesh
generation that is consistent with the input sequence.
We then initialize the object pose in each frame of the
input sequence. Given an RGB frame Ik and the gener-
ated 3D textured mesh V , our goal is to estimate the object
pose ˆξk for the frame, which transforms the object from its
canonical space to the camera space. ˆξk includes 3D ro-
tation Rk ∈ SO(3) and translation Tk ∈ R3. Previous
works [5, 15] render 2D mask Mrender from the 3D mesh
and compare it with the input object mask M for optimiza-
tion:
Lproj = ∥Mrender − M ∥. (6)

may produce similar 2D masks, as shown in Fig. 3.

frames into calibrated multi-view images in object-centric
coordinates for implicit representation optimization. How-

struction quality. BARF [28] demonstrates that jointly op-
timizing the implicit representation and camera poses miti-
gates pose noise. Building on this, we treat camera poses as
optimizable parameters for joint shape-pose optimization.

3.4. Pose Outlier Voting with Sampson Distance
Although the poses from initialization can be refined by
joint optimization in many cases, severe occlusions between
the hand and object can lead to significant deviations in the
initial poses and these pose outliers result in erroneous joint
optimization and hinder the refinement. To address this is-
sue, we propose a pose outlier voting strategy to detect and
correct the poses.
One solution to find the outliers is to use per-frame ren-
dering quality PSNR [19] to indicate inaccuracies in the
corresponding camera pose ξk. However, we observe that
inaccurate camera poses can also affect neighboring view-
points due to the implicit network learning incorrect shape
and texture in these regions, which can lead to erroneous
assessments when using the PSNR of neighboring rendered
images.
Instead, we additionally utilize the previously obtained
correspondence cues as a metric to help detect erroneous
poses. Given pairs of 2D matched points pi ∈ Ii and pj ∈
Ij, along with the fundamental matrix F obtained from the
current camera poses ξi and ξj, we compute the Sampson
distance:

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation Details
We use SAM-v2 [37] and StableNormal [55] to generate
object masks and monocular normals for each frame. The
number of generated meshes, NV , is set to 12, and ren-
der the textured meshes using PyTorch3D [36]. Semantic
features are extracted with DINO-ViT-B/14. We adopt the
same architecture for the implicit network as NeuS [49].
During training, we employ the Adam optimizer [24] with
a learning rate of 5e − 4 and a cosine decay schedule with
α = 0 .05. We sample 1024 rays per batch over a total
of 120k iterations. At 20k iterations, we detect erroneous
poses and reinitialize the pose and geometry MLPs. Train-
ing for 100 frames takes about 1 hour for pose initialization
and 8 hours for reconstruction on an NVIDIA RTX 3090
GPU. Our implementation is based on PyTorch.

4.3. Metrics and Baselines
Metrics. Following previous works [9, 18, 56], we use
the Chamfer Distance (CD) to evaluate object reconstruc-
tion quality and the F-score (in percentage) to assess local
shape details. Specifically, we apply ICP to register the re-
constructed mesh with the ground-truth. For ground-truth
meshes with known scale ( e.g., HO3D, HOPE), we com-
pute the Chamfer Distance in squared centimeters and the
F-score at 5mm (F5) and 10mm (F10). For HOD, where
the scale is unknown, meshes are normalized to unit size,
and only the Chamfer Distance is reported. Additionally,
we evaluate pose accuracy using standard visual odometry
metrics [62], including Absolute Trajectory Error ( ATE)
and Relative Pose Error ( RPE). ATE measures the differ-
ence between estimated and ground truth camera positions,
while RPE quantifies relative rotation (RPE r) and trans-
lation (RPE t) errors between image pairs. We align the
predicted trajectory with ground truth via Umeyama align-
ment [48], reporting RPEr in degrees, RPEt in centimeters,
and ATE in meters.
Baselines. We compare our method with several existing
approaches, including HOLD [9], the state-of-the-art for re-
constructing hand-object interaction from monocular RGB
video, which relies on SfM to initialize the object pose. As
the method fails to reconstruct the banana and scissors in
HO3D, HOLD evaluates reconstruction quality by adding
noise to the ground truth poses for initialization on these
two objects. The evaluation of HOLD in Tab. 1 adopts their
settings. When calculating the pose trajectory metric, we
exclude these parts. IHOI [56], a learning-based method
for monocular-image in-hand object reconstruction, trained
on sequences from HO3D and other datasets. We eval-
uate IHOI on each frame of the sequence and report the
average results. Hampali et al. [13] shares the same set-
tings as ours. Without available code, we follow HOLD
and compare against their released 3D models reconstructed
by their method on the same HO3D sequences, excluding
the banana and scissors due to their known failure cases.
In two-hand scenarios, where no available prior work has
addressed this challenge, we compare with BARF [28].
For a fair comparison, we provide initialization poses from
COLMAP, similar to HOLD but without hand refinement,
referring to this baseline as COLMAP-BARF.

4.4. State-of-the-art Comparsion
Single-handed object reconstruction. We visualize the re-
constructed object meshes and estimated pose trajectories
in Fig. 4. The learning-based method IHOI can predict
coarse shapes on seen data but lacks fine details. It also fails
to reconstruct unseen objects from the HOD dataset, gen-
erating invalid meshes. HOLD can accurately reconstruct
geometry for objects with rich features, but its performance
is significantly limited by its object pose initialization via
SfM, as shown in the trajectories. For example, SfM gets
confused by the similar appearance of the two sides of the
drill, resulting in completely incorrect pose initialization.
Additionally, SfM struggles to initialize correct poses for
texture-less or thin objects, leading to poor-quality recon-
struction results. In contrast, by incorporating the gener-
ated prior, our method achieves superior geometry quality
and accurate pose trajectories, even for challenging objects
like scissors, the cat, and the rubber duck.
This is consistent with quantitative results in Tab. 1. Our
method significantly outperforms comparison methods on
reconstruction metrics on both datasets, particularly on the

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we present a method to reconstruct high-
quality hand-held objects from monocular RGB video with
dynamic interactions. The key insights include incorporat-
ing generated 3D priors and enforcing semantic consistency
for coarse pose initialization, which significantly supports
the optimization of the object’s implicit representation. We
also introduce a pose outlier voting strategy to further en-
hance both reconstruction quality and pose accuracy. Our
method consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across
multiple datasets.
Limitations and discussion: Despite promising results,
our method has certain limitations. Its effectiveness de-
pends on the quality of generated priors, which may not
perform well with unique objects that current generators
struggle to represent accurately. Additionally, DINO may
produce nearly identical features for different sides of an
object, leading to incorrect pose estimations in some cases.
Advances in semantic correspondences [60] may help miti-
gate this issue. Nevertheless, we believe this work provides
valuable insights for reconstructing objects from unposed
multi-view images.
